\begin{abstract}
Vision-language models (VLMs) are now used as judges to rank image generators, to curate multimodal preference data and to supply rewards for reinforcement learning. A judge that is wrong 30\% of the time is still useful, provided we know \emph{which} 30\%.
For text judges, order-symmetric confidence (query both orders and average the preference) combined with conformal thresholds gives selective judgments with a guaranteed error rate \citep{badshah2026scope,jung2024trust}.
We ask whether this recipe survives the move to vision. We use \TBD{10} public multimodal judge benchmarks (\TBD{10.9k} pairs), Place Pulse~2.0 street-view perception, and three open VLM judges. We find three things.
\textbf{(i)} Order-symmetric confidence still ranks errors, but weakly (AUROC .60--.64 on Place Pulse, \TBD{range} on the benchmarks).
\textbf{(ii)} For some judges it saturates: 67\% of Place Pulse pairs reach $\dpconf\ge.95$ for Qwen3-VL-8B, and only 70\% of those are right. At tight risk targets a conformal threshold therefore certifies almost nothing.
\textbf{(iii)} It barely separates pairs that humans call a tie from strict pairs (AUROC .54).
We propose \eaglev{}, an escalation ladder (pairwise $\rightarrow$ pointwise $\rightarrow$ stronger judge $\rightarrow$ human). Its thresholds are calibrated jointly by fixed-sequence testing, so the error among all machine-decided pairs is at most $\alpha$ with probability $1-\delta$. Inserting a cheap \emph{protocol} rung before any model or human rung raises certified automation at the same risk, for example from 32\% to 44\% for Qwen3.5-9B on Place Pulse at $\alpha=.30$ (\TBD{benchmarks}).
For rankings, we fit prediction-powered Bradley--Terry/Davidson models with confidence-routed human labels. We prove that the gain from any judge is capped by the share of vote variance that is predictable from the pair. On Place Pulse, after we remove the duplicate submissions that make up nearly all repeated votes, even a human-fitted Bradley--Terry model explains only 2--10\% of held-out vote variance. Judges can therefore help rank images but cannot replace individual votes.
\end{abstract}
