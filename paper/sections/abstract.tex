\begin{abstract}
Vision-language models (VLMs) are now used as judges to rank image generators, to curate multimodal preference data and to supply rewards for reinforcement learning. A judge that is wrong 30\% of the time is still useful, provided we know \emph{which} 30\%.
For text judges, order-symmetric confidence (query both orders and average the preference) combined with conformal thresholds gives selective judgments with a guaranteed error rate \citep{badshah2026scope,jung2024trust}.
We ask whether this recipe survives the move to vision. We use ten public multimodal judge benchmarks (10.9k pairs), Place Pulse~2.0 street-view perception, three open 7--9B VLM judges and a 32B judge.
\textbf{(i)} Order-symmetric confidence still ranks errors, but weakly: AUROC .57--.77 (median .66), no better on text responses than on generated images. It is strong only where labels are near-deterministic, such as safety (AUROC up to 1.0), and fails on contested or adversarial pairs.
\textbf{(ii)} Saturation is judge-specific: a median 54\% of pairs reach the confidence ceiling for Qwen3-VL-8B against 11\% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Reading the same calls as log-odds recovers certified coverage exactly where saturation bites (50\%$\to$90\% on VL-RewardBench).
\textbf{(iii)} Confidence barely detects human ties (AUROC .54--.58 on the three large tie sets).
We then calibrate escalation ladders (pairwise $\rightarrow$ pointwise $\rightarrow$ 32B judge $\rightarrow$ human) jointly by fixed-sequence testing over a small family of paths, so the error among all machine-decided pairs is at most $\alpha$ with probability $1-\delta$ and unhelpful rungs are switched off by the data. Escalation adds 3--9 points of certified automation on average and more than 50 points on some datasets, but no rung order dominates, and a 4$\times$ larger judge helps only the weakest small judge. On hard image-preference sets (MMRB2, ImageRewardDB) almost nothing can be certified at $\alpha\le.20$.
For leaderboards, prediction-powered Bradley--Terry with judge soft labels matches human-only rankings that use about 1.5$\times$ as many votes on GenAI-Arena text-to-image; routing human labels by judge uncertainty does not help. A simple bound shows that the gain is capped by the share of vote variance predictable from the pair, which on Place Pulse, after removing the duplicate submissions that make up nearly all repeated votes, is only 2--10\%.
\end{abstract}
