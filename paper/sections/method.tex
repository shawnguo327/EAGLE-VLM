\section{\eaglev{}: Risk-Controlled Escalation}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Ladder.}
\eaglev{} processes each pair through a sequence of rungs $r=1,\dots,R$. Each rung has a verdict $\hat y_r$ and a confidence $c_r$, plus a final human rung.
In our instantiation:
(1) the pairwise judge with confidence $\lambda$;
(2) the same model's pointwise margin $|m|$, which costs two extra forward passes;
(3) optionally a stronger judge, for example an API model in pairwise mode with its own order-averaged confidence;
(4) a human.
A pair stops at the first rung $r$ with $c_r\ge t_r$. The thresholds $t=(t_1,\dots,t_{R-1})$ define the system.
The selective risk is the error rate among machine-decided pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R(t)=\Pr\big[\hat y_{r(x)}\neq y \;\big|\; r(x)<R\big],
\end{equation}
where $r(x)$ is the stopping rung. We want to maximise automation $\Pr[r(x)<R]$ (or minimise expected cost) subject to $\mathcal R(t)\le\alpha$.

\paragraph{Calibration with a guarantee.}
Let $\mathcal D_{\rm cal}$ be $n$ human-labelled pairs, exchangeable with test pairs.
Following Learn-then-Test \citep{angelopoulos2021learn}, we fix in advance a path of threshold vectors $t^{(1)}\succeq t^{(2)}\succeq\dots\succeq t^{(K)}$, ordered from strict to lenient.
\emph{Path family.} A single fixed path forces every rung to accept pairs, even a rung that is weaker than the one before it. We therefore use a small family $\mathcal F$ of equal-quantile paths, indexed by a mixing ratio $\rho_r\in\{0,1\}$ for each rung after the first. Along path $\rho$, at step $k$ rung 1 accepts its top $\kappa_k$ share of confidences and rung $r$ its top $\min(1,\rho_r\kappa_k)$ share, with $\kappa_k$ increasing from a start value to 100\%; $\rho_r=0$ switches rung $r$ off. The thresholds depend only on the (unlabelled) confidences, never on the labels.
For each path and each $k$ we test $H_k:\mathcal R(t^{(k)})>\alpha$ with the one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound \citep{clopper1934use} on the number of errors among machine-decided calibration pairs, at level $\delta/|\mathcal F|$. We walk each path and stop at its first $k$ that fails to reject, then deploy the certified threshold vector with the lowest calibration cost across paths. The start value of $\kappa$ is the smallest coverage at which $n\kappa$ pairs could certify $\alpha$ even with zero errors.
\begin{proposition}[Fixed-sequence guarantee]\label{prop:ltt}
Let $\hat t$ be the deployed thresholds. Then $\Pr\big[\mathcal R(\hat t)\le\alpha\big]\ge1-\delta$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent Within each path this is the fixed-sequence-testing instance of Learn-then-Test: each test is valid, and the walk stops at the first non-rejection. A union bound over the $|\mathcal F|$ paths makes every certified vector valid simultaneously with probability $1-\delta$, so choosing among them by cost is safe.
The guarantee covers the whole ladder jointly. Adding a rung therefore cannot break validity; it can only change how many pairs are certified, and the family lets the calibration data switch an unhelpful rung off at the price of a factor $|\mathcal F|$ in $\delta$ ($|\mathcal F|=2$ for two machine rungs, 4 for three).

\paragraph{Protocol rungs and model rungs.}
Trust-or-Escalate escalates across \emph{models} \citep{jung2024trust}. With VLM judges, errors are correlated across model families (\cref{sec:diagnostic}), so a second model may repeat the first one's mistake. A pointwise rung has different failure modes: it is immune to position bias and to the forced-choice bias \citep{khan2026seeing}, and it costs two forward passes of the same model. It is also usually less accurate (Finding~4). \eaglev{} offers both kinds of rung and lets calibration decide which to use; \cref{sec:experiments} reports when each pays off.

\paragraph{Ties.}
With the Davidson model \citep{davidson1970extending}, human ties enter the ranking likelihood (\cref{sec:ranking}). For selective risk we exclude human ties, because no judge in our study ever outputs a tie (Finding~3); counting a strict verdict on a tie as half an error would add a constant to every method's risk.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\eaglev{} calibration for one path $\rho$ (run for every $\rho\in\mathcal F$ at level $\delta/|\mathcal F|$; deploy the cheapest certified result)}\label{alg:cal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require calibration pairs with rung confidences $c_{r,i}$ and errors $e_{r,i}$; target $\alpha$; level $\delta$; coverage grid $\kappa_1<\dots<\kappa_K$
\State $t^\star\gets(\infty,\dots,\infty)$ \Comment{everything to humans}
\For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
  \State $t_r\gets$ the $(1-\min(1,\rho_r\kappa_k))$-quantile of $\{c_{r,i}\}$ for each machine rung $r$ ($\rho_1=1$; $t_r=\infty$ if $\rho_r=0$)
  \State route each pair to its first rung with $c_{r,i}\ge t_r$; let $m$ be the number of machine-decided pairs and $E$ their errors
  \If{$m>0$ \textbf{and} $\mathrm{CP}^{+}_{1-\delta/|\mathcal F|}(E,m)\le\alpha$} \State $t^\star\gets t$ \Else\ \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $t^\star$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
