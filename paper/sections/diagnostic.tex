\section{Does Order-Symmetric Confidence Transfer to Vision?}\label{sec:diagnostic}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
\Cref{tab:data} lists the \TBD{ten} public judge benchmarks we converted to a common pairwise format. We subsample to at most 1{,}500 pairs per dataset, stratified by subset.
They cover \emph{response judging}, where the candidates are text answers about images (VL-RewardBench, Multimodal RewardBench, MM-RLHF-RewardBench, MLLM-as-a-Judge pairwise), and \emph{image judging} (GenAI-Arena, MJ-Bench, MMRB2 text-to-image and editing, ImageRewardDB, Q-Bench2, EditReward-Bench).
GenAI-Arena, MLLM-as-a-Judge and EditReward-Bench contain human ties (\TBD{868} in total), which we use to study tie behaviour.
Place Pulse 2.0 contributes 1{,}500 strict votes and 360 tie votes across six attributes.

\paragraph{Judges.}
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen3vl}, Qwen3.5-9B (natively multimodal) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen25vl}, run in non-thinking mode with first-token readout. \todo{add one API judge (GPT-5-mini or Gemini Flash) on a 300-pair subset per dataset as the ``stronger judge'' rung.}

\paragraph{Finding 1: confidence ranks errors, weakly.}
On Place Pulse, accuracy rises from .51--.57 in the lowest $\dpconf$ quintile to .78--.81 in the highest for all three judges. AUROC is .60--.64.

\paragraph{Finding 2: saturation is judge-specific, and log-odds does not fix it.}
The share of pairs with $\dpconf\ge.95$ is 67\% for Qwen3-VL-8B, 27\% for Qwen3.5-9B and 11\% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The ECE of $(1+\dpconf)/2$ follows the same order: .25, .20, .11.
Only 70\% of Qwen3-VL-8B's saturated pairs are correct.
Reading the same two calls as order-averaged log-odds $\lambda$ does still rank errors \emph{inside} the saturated mass (AUROC .62 for Qwen3-VL-8B). However, $\dpconf$ already orders these pairs almost identically, so overall AUROC (.640 vs .639) and certified coverage do not change.
Saturation is therefore a problem of \emph{scale} (calibration), not of \emph{ranking}. It matters for any use that reads $(1+\dpconf)/2$ as a probability, such as soft labels for PPI or DPO, and not for threshold-based selection.

\paragraph{Finding 3: confidence barely detects human ties.}
Humans mark 13\% of Place Pulse comparisons as ``equal''. Low $\dpconf$ separates these tie votes from strict votes with AUROC .54 for all three judges, and when offered a tie option the judges never choose it.

\emph{Note on human disagreement.} We first planned to measure human disagreement on Place Pulse pairs with several votes, as in earlier work. An audit of the vote timestamps (\cref{app:ppaudit}) shows this is not possible. Of 18{,}630 consecutive repeat votes on the same pair, 81\% arrive within 10 seconds with the same left/right layout and agree 91\% of the time, so they are duplicate submissions. Only 98 repeats are a day or more apart, and those agree only 61\% of the time. None of the 720 ``multi-vote'' pairs in our pilot has two independent votes after deduplication.

\paragraph{Finding 4: pointwise is a different, not worse, channel.}
On Place Pulse, pointwise scoring alone (.650--.666) matches or beats pairwise (.645--.649). The two protocols disagree on 15--17\% of pairs. On those pairs pointwise is right 50--57\% of the time, so a coin-flip set is being exposed rather than corrected. Errors are correlated across judges (.65--.73). A second protocol is therefore a more independent check than a second VLM; this motivates the ladder in \cref{sec:method}.
