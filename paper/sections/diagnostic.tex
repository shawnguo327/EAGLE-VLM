\section{Does Order-Symmetric Confidence Transfer to Vision?}\label{sec:diagnostic}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
\Cref{tab:data} lists the ten public judge benchmarks we converted to a common pairwise format. We subsample to at most 1{,}500 pairs per dataset, stratified by subset.
They cover \emph{response judging}, where the candidates are text answers about images (VL-RewardBench, Multimodal RewardBench, MM-RLHF-RewardBench, MLLM-as-a-Judge pairwise), and \emph{image judging} (GenAI-Arena, MJ-Bench, MMRB2 text-to-image and editing, ImageRewardDB, Q-Bench2, EditReward-Bench).
GenAI-Arena, MLLM-as-a-Judge and EditReward-Bench contain human ties (868 in total), which we use to study tie behaviour.
Place Pulse 2.0 contributes 1{,}500 strict votes and 360 tie votes across six attributes.

\paragraph{Judges.}
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen3vl}, Qwen3.5-9B (natively multimodal) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct \citep{bai2025qwen25vl}, run in non-thinking mode with first-token readout. Each judge sees every pair in both orders (pairwise) and scores each candidate on a 1--9 scale (pointwise). Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, run pairwise only, is the stronger-judge rung in \cref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Finding 1: confidence ranks errors, weakly, and equally for responses and images.}
Across the 30 benchmark--judge cells, pairwise accuracy ranges from .59 to .89 (median .72) and the AUROC of $\dpconf$ for predicting a correct verdict from .57 to .77 (median .66). Accuracy in the most confident quintile (.70--1.00) is always above that in the least confident quintile (.50--.76).
We expected confidence to work better when judging text responses, where labels are often verifiable, than when judging generated images. It does not: for every judge, mean AUROC is .65--.68 on the four response benchmarks and .65--.68 on the six image benchmarks.
The hard cases are text-to-image and editing preferences between close candidates (MMRB2, ImageRewardDB), with accuracy .59--.64 and AUROC .57--.62 for all three judges. On Place Pulse AUROC is .60--.64.

\paragraph{Finding 2: saturation is judge-specific; log-odds helps only where it bites.}
The median share of benchmark pairs with $\dpconf\ge.95$ is 54\% for Qwen3-VL-8B, 24\% for Qwen3.5-9B and 11\% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Place Pulse: 67\%, 27\%, 11\%). The median ECE of $(1+\dpconf)/2$ follows the same order: .14, .08, .07.
Saturation does not mean the verdicts are right: only 70\% of Qwen3-VL-8B's saturated Place Pulse pairs are correct.
Reading the same two calls as order-averaged log-odds $\lambda$ never lowers AUROC and raises it by more than .01 in 10 of 33 cells. The gain in \emph{certified coverage} is concentrated where saturation is heaviest. For Qwen3-VL-8B at $\alpha=.30$, coverage rises from 50\% to 90\% on VL-RewardBench and from 61\% to 85\% on MLLM-as-a-Judge. Elsewhere the two scores certify similar sets, and $\lambda$ is worse in a minority of cells (e.g.\ Qwen3.5-9B on Q-Bench2).
Saturation is therefore mainly a problem of \emph{scale}. It matters for any use that reads $(1+\dpconf)/2$ as a probability, such as soft labels for PPI or DPO, and for thresholding only when most pairs sit at the ceiling. We use $\lambda$ as the pairwise confidence from here on.

\paragraph{Finding 3: confidence barely detects human ties.}
Humans mark 13\% of Place Pulse comparisons as ``equal''. Low $\dpconf$ separates these tie votes from strict votes with AUROC .54 for all three judges, and when offered a tie option the judges never choose it.
The benchmarks with human ties show the same picture: AUROC .54--.58 on GenAI-Arena and MLLM-as-a-Judge, and .63--.67 on the 18 ties of EditReward-Bench.
\emph{Note on human disagreement.} We first planned to measure human disagreement on Place Pulse pairs with several votes, as in earlier work. An audit of the vote timestamps (\cref{app:ppaudit}) shows this is not possible. Of 18{,}630 consecutive repeat votes on the same pair, 81\% arrive within 10 seconds with the same left/right layout and agree 91\% of the time, so they are duplicate submissions. Only 98 repeats are a day or more apart, and those agree only 61\% of the time. None of the 720 ``multi-vote'' pairs in our pilot has two independent votes after deduplication.

\paragraph{Finding 4: pointwise is a weaker, partly independent channel.}
On Place Pulse, pointwise scoring alone (.650--.666) matches or beats pairwise (.645--.649). On the benchmarks it is usually weaker, by up to 8 points (e.g.\ MJ-Bench .72 vs .79 for Qwen3-VL-8B, Multimodal RB .71 vs .75).
The two protocols disagree on 15--17\% of Place Pulse pairs, and there pointwise is right 50--57\% of the time, so a coin-flip set is exposed rather than corrected. Errors are correlated across judges (.65--.73 on Place Pulse).
Whether a second protocol is worth an escalation step is therefore an empirical question per dataset, which \cref{sec:experiments} answers.
